Most people stuck on a decision don't need more facts. They need to separate what they know from what they're assuming, name what matters, and find the one thing the decision turns on.

Know. Weigh. See.

Ten questions, one at a time. Then a report. The decision remains yours.

01 · Know

What is true, what is assumed, what is unknown?

Everything you share is sorted into facts you could verify today, beliefs you're treating as true, and unknowns that could change the picture.

  • "The new job pays $110,000." Fact.
  • "I'll get promoted within two years." Belief.
  • "How many hours I'll actually work." Unknown.
02 · Weigh

What matters, and what are you willing to risk?

Six months against five years. The regret of acting against the regret of not acting. And the question most tools skip: if you dislike it, how easy is it to undo?

03 · See

What changes the decision?

You predict first. Then Clarity argues the other side, tests how sensitive your call is to each assumption, and finds the one question most worth answering before you decide.

Clarity never pretends certainty. Seven rules it won't break.

  1. 01Never manufacture precision.
  2. 02Never hide uncertainty.
  3. 03Never confuse correlation with causation.
  4. 04Never pretend preferences are facts.
  5. 05Never make the decision for you.
  6. 06Never bury the assumption driving the result.
  7. 07Never ask a question unless the answer could matter.
