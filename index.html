How Clarity thinks.

You build the decision, one confirmed step at a time. Clarity does the arithmetic and shows you which uncertainty matters. No score. No verdict.

  1. What you're deciding, and the ways it could go. Two or three, in plain words.
  2. What could matter. Pick from a library, or add your own. Then say how much each one matters.
  3. Your read, and how sure you are. Stored separately. Guessing is a real answer.
  4. The Pivot. The uncertainty that would change the result, weighed by how much it matters and how unsure you are. Shown only when there is one.

Here's the thing you couldn't see from inside the problem.

Clarity doesn't remove uncertainty. It shows you which uncertainty matters, and what to do about it.
